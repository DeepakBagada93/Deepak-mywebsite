# Research Dossier — AI Email Automation for Agencies

## Market problem

Agencies have two conflicting constraints: outbound must be personal, and delivery is capacity-constrained. A founder can write five excellent emails a day, not fifty. A junior can send fifty generic emails that damage the domain. The result is either slow pipeline or weak personalization.

AI closes the gap only if the risky part is contained. The valuable system is not “AI writes and sends email.” It is **AI drafts, code checks, a human approves, and the system handles the follow-up timing.**

## Cost of the manual process

| Task | Time per lead | Weekly cost at 50 leads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research trigger and pain | 8 min | 6.7 hours |
| Write first email | 10 min | 8.3 hours |
| Write two follow-ups | 8 min | 6.7 hours |
| Track who to follow up with | 5 min | 4.2 hours |
| QA links, spam words, compliance | 3 min | 2.5 hours |
| **Total** | **34 min** | **28.4 hours** |

At a $60/hour internal cost, that is roughly **$1,700 per week** of capacity. The system removes most of the drafting and tracking time while keeping a 60–90 second human review per email.

## Positioning

> The agency-safe AI email system: AI does the first draft, code enforces the rules, and a human makes the send decision.

## Why the human-in-the-loop is the feature

- Agencies sell judgment. An autonomous sender undermines the founder voice.
- A single hallucinated claim can damage a client relationship.
- Approval links make the review take under two minutes.
- The audit trail (draft, token, decision, timestamps) is useful for client reporting.
