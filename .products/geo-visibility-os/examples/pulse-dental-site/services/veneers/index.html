Veneers in Austin, TX

Pulse Dental Studio provides porcelain veneers for adults in Austin, TX. This page explains who is a good candidate, what happens at each visit, which factors change the cost, how to care for veneers, and the limits of what a veneer can fix. The goal is to help you decide with a clear treatment plan rather than a promise.

By Dr. Maya Patel, DDS · Published February 18, 2026 · Updated October 12, 2026

What do veneers change, and what do they not?

Veneers are thin porcelain shells bonded to the front of the teeth. They can change shape, length, shade and small gaps. They do not fix a bad bite, replace a missing tooth or cure gum disease. If the bite is unstable, we treat that first, because a veneer on a moving bite is likely to chip. If the enamel is thin or the tooth is heavily filled, a crown may be the better option. The consultation compares those choices on your own teeth, with photographs and a shade guide, so the decision is based on your case rather than a generic package.

How does the two-visit process work?

The first visit is a 60-minute exam with photographs, a bite check and a written plan. If you proceed, we prepare the teeth, take digital impressions and place temporary veneers. The lab builds the porcelain, and the second visit is the bond and bite adjustment. Some cases need a third visit for a refine or a remake. In 2025, 88% of our veneer cases finished in two visits, and 12% needed a third visit for a lab adjustment. We tell you that range before you start so a third visit is not a surprise. Sedation is available for appropriate cases, and the team reviews your health history first.

What changes the cost?

The number of teeth is the biggest driver. Material and lab choice, the complexity of the bite, temporary veneers and sedation each add cost. A quote should list the per-tooth fee, the lab fee, the imaging fee and the follow-up policy. It should also say what happens if a veneer needs a remake. In 2026, 71% of our new patients said the written breakdown was the main reason they trusted the plan. Ask for that document before any preparation, and compare it with at least one other provider.

Who is not a good candidate?

Veneers are not the right choice for someone with untreated gum disease, active decay, severe grinding without a guard, or a bite that needs orthodontic correction first. We also do not recommend them for a patient who wants a guarantee that the result will never change. Porcelain is durable, but it is not indestructible. If your case is outside our scope, we refer you to a specialist and explain why. That referral is part of the service, not a failure of it.

How do you care for veneers?

Brush and floss as usual, avoid biting hard objects, and wear a guard if you grind. Attend the scheduled reviews so we can check the margins and the bite. Chips can usually be repaired, and a remake is sometimes covered by the lab warranty. Ask for the warranty terms in writing. Our own limit is clear: we can describe the process, the range of outcomes and the cost drivers, but we cannot promise a specific cosmetic result before the exam.
