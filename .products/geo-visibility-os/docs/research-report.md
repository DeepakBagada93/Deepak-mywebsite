# US market research — which product to build next

Date: 2026-10-29 · Author: Deepak Bagada Systems · Scope: US buyers, digital skill products, one-time purchase · Evidence type: public web research, dated

## 1. Decision

Build **GEO Visibility OS** — a US-facing AI search visibility audit and fix system for SEO agencies, consultants and B2B/SMB marketing teams.

Why this one:

- The pain is urgent and measurable: AI answers are taking clicks, and buyers are asking agencies for GEO work now.
- The buyer already pays for audits: one-time AI visibility audits sell for **$500–$5,000**, and agency retainers run **$1,500–$50,000/month**.
- Search demand is rising on both sides: agencies report GEO in **55% of client briefs** once they offer it, versus **8%** for agencies that do not.
- The tool market validates willingness to pay: tracking tools run **$29–$654/month**, with a category average near **$337/month**.
- The gap is execution, not another dashboard: **71% of agencies offering GEO spend more time explaining it than doing it**, and only **41% can turn data into action**.
- It fits the existing portfolio: no current product is a US GEO audit/fix system, and the repo already has the distribution and product conventions.

The product is **not** an "AI visibility score" dashboard and **not** an `llms.txt` hack. It is a deterministic audit, a buyer-prompt panel, a 90-day fix roadmap, schema and content briefs, a citation-target plan, a measurement plan and a white-label client report.

### Why GEO beats the runner-up

AI receptionist is a bigger operational pain, but a credible product needs telephony, call flows, CRM integrations and live testing. A prompt pack alone would be easy to copy and hard to prove. GEO can be audited from a website snapshot with deterministic checks, produces a client-ready artifact, and is bought by agencies that already sell audits. That makes it a better fit for a zero-dependency skill bundle.

### Why not another UGC product

We already ship UGC Ad Studio. The category is crowded, the buyer expects renders, and differentiation would be cosmetic. GEO is a different budget line: organic/search strategy, not paid creative.

## 5. Competitive gap

| Alternative | What it does | What it misses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monitoring tools (Profound, Peec, Otterly, Athena) | Track mentions, citations, share of voice | No deterministic site-readiness audit, no fix roadmap, no content/schema/client pack |
| Agency audits | Human analysis and a report | Expensive, slow, inconsistent between juniors, not repeatable |
| Gumroad/Notion checklists | A list of checks | No scanner, no generated schema/content briefs, no audit score, no tests |
| Free AI visibility scanners | A score and a lead form | Shallow, no implementation artifacts, often incentivised to sell a retainer |
| Classic SEO audits | Crawl, keywords, backlinks | Miss AI crawler access, entity consistency, answer-first extraction and citation-source gaps |

**Positioning:** "The audit is the product, not the dashboard." A deterministic readiness score plus the exact artifacts needed to fix it, usable under the agency's own brand.

## 7. Differentiation to build

1. **Deterministic 40-check audit** with a weighted score and a verdict, not a vibes score.
2. **No snake oil:** explicitly rejects `llms.txt` as a visibility lever and says so in the report, backed by 2026 crawler data.
3. **Crawl reality:** checks the specific AI user agents, including the training-vs-search distinction (`GPTBot` vs `OAI-SearchBot`).
4. **Entity consistency:** brand, service, location, author and `sameAs` checks across pages.
5. **Answer-first content briefs:** six briefs built from buyer prompts, with extractable answer blocks, evidence requirements and schema.
6. **Schema pack:** valid JSON-LD for Organization, Service/Product, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList and Article.
7. **Citation-source map:** the pages and communities AI actually cites, not a generic backlink list.
8. **White-label client report:** agency name, score, findings, 90-day roadmap, measurement plan.
9. **Honest measurement:** GSC generative AI report, Bing citation share, GA4 AI referrals, manual prompt panel, and modelled-not-guaranteed language.
10. **Zero dependencies and offline tests:** works on a laptop, no API keys, no vendor lock-in.

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Evidence | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market is noisy and full of rebranded SEO | Google says GEO is still SEO; Scribewise says 71% of agencies over-explain | Lead with deterministic fixes and honest limits |
| AI answers are volatile | arXiv 2026: measure a distribution, not one answer | Prompt panel with repeated runs and dates; no single-score guarantees |
| `llms.txt` hype | 97% of files unread (Ahrefs); 0.1% of AI crawler requests (Otterly) | Explicitly reject it as a lever; include it only as optional agent-readiness |
| Tool prices change | Profound/Otterly/Peec plans shift | Treat prices as dated benchmarks; no hard dependency |
| Buyers expect monitoring | Tools dominate the category | Position as audit + fix system; integrate with whichever tracker they use |
| Cheap template competition | £39 Gumroad kit | Ship working scripts, generated artifacts and tests, not a PDF |

## 9. Low-cost validation experiment

1. Build the product and the sample audit (this release).
2. Publish a free "AI Search Readiness Check" landing page using the scanner on a public URL, email-gated.
3. Offer a $99 launch audit to the first 20 agencies; deliver the pack manually.
4. Track: landing-page conversion, audit purchase rate, time-to-deliver, and how many buyers ask for monitoring.
5. Kill criterion: fewer than 3 paid audits from 100 qualified visitors in 30 days. Pivot to the agency white-label offer or the AI receptionist pack.
