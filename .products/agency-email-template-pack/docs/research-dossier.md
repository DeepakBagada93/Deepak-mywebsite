# Research Dossier — Agency Email Template Pack

## Market context

Agencies and service businesses sell trust before they sell deliverables. The email moments before and after a call carry a disproportionate share of that trust. Yet most agencies reuse the same generic proposal email and a cold template copied from a 2019 blog post.

The commercial gap is not “more templates.” It is **templates mapped to the agency revenue moments**, with copy that sounds like a founder rather than a marketing department.

## Buyer pain points (observed in agency operations)

| Moment | Failure mode | Revenue impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First cold touch | No trigger, generic pitch, calendar link | Low reply rate, domain damage |
| Discovery call | No recap, next step unclear | Deal stalls in “let me think” |
| Proposal follow-up | Either silence or aggressive chasing | Discount pressure, ghosting |
| Kickoff | Client waits for access instructions | Early dissatisfaction |
| Proof distribution | Case study buried on a blog | No inbound referrals |
| Win-back | Old clients forgotten | Missed repeat revenue |
| Referral | Awkward ask or no ask | Zero word-of-mouth compounding |

## Competitive landscape

| Alternative | Price | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic template marketplaces | $9–$49 | Pretty but no strategy or copy system |
| Email design tools | $99+/month | Requires design skill and copywriting |
| Agency newsletters/courses | $200–$2,000 | Teaching, not ready-to-send assets |
| Freelance copywriter | $300–$1,500/email | Expensive per asset, inconsistent voice |

## Positioning

> The eight emails an agency actually needs, written for the buyer moment, not for a portfolio screenshot.

The pack replaces at least one freelance copywriting engagement ($300+) and one missed follow-up. The price is intentionally below the cost of a single missed proposal.

## Evidence limits

- No claim is made about open, reply, or conversion rates.
- Benchmarks vary by list quality, offer, domain reputation, and market.
- The reference campaign uses illustrative company names and metrics; buyers must replace them with verified results.
